About

My academic trajectory began in industrial engineering rather than computer science. My undergraduate research addressed the optimisation of physical systems, including a CNC hot-wire foam-cutting apparatus and automobile inbound logistics simulated in FlexSim, contexts in which a model's value is determined by its performance on an operating production line rather than by its fit to historical data. This standard of practical validation has continued to inform my approach to data science.

I am currently completing a Master of Science in Data Science at Adelaide University. My research there addresses a narrower, and I believe more consequential, question than which model performs best: namely, which models can be trusted, under what conditions, and for what duration. This entails the design of leakage-controlled evaluation protocols, the use of statistical significance testing in place of single-run comparisons, and the reporting of calibration and temporal stability alongside conventional accuracy measures.

I intend to extend this line of inquiry at the doctoral level, applying trustworthiness evaluation to foundation models for time-series forecasting and to safety-critical domains such as clinical signal classification, where a confidently incorrect prediction carries greater risk than an appropriately hedged one.

Research interests

Trustworthy foundation models

Evaluation of time-series foundation models (Chronos, TimesFM, Moirai) against classical and deep baselines for robustness, calibration, and temporal stability, in addition to point accuracy.

Time-series forecasting

Leakage-controlled, reproducible forecasting pipelines across noisy, non-stationary domains such as financial and energy data.

Healthcare artificial intelligence

Patient-independent evaluation of clinical classifiers, with attention to minority-class performance in contexts where errors carry the highest cost.

Reproducible ML evaluation

Statistical rigour in model comparison, including significance testing, uncertainty quantification, and evaluation design that resists silent data leakage.

Forecasting for critical civic systems

Extending this trustworthiness evaluation methodology beyond healthcare to other decision-critical domains, including supply chain resilience, disaster early-warning systems, and housing affordability, where forecasting errors carry direct social and economic consequences.

Research & projects

Trustworthy Foundation Models for Time-Series Forecasting

2026

A leakage-controlled, reproducible forecasting framework comparing Chronos, TimesFM, classical models (ARIMA, SARIMA, Holt-Winters, exponential smoothing), LSTM, Prophet, and naive baselines across Bitcoin and electricity datasets. Models are evaluated on accuracy, statistical significance, robustness, temporal stability, and uncertainty calibration, treating trustworthiness as multidimensional rather than as a single score.

MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Heartbeat Classification

2026

A patient-independent pipeline classifying four AAMI heartbeat classes from the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database (PhysioNet), comparing Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and CNN models. Evaluation uses per-class precision/recall/F1, precision-recall AUC, and McNemar's test to compare minority-class recall statistically, relating model complexity to minority-class performance.
